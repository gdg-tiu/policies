# GDG on Campus — Community Code of Conduct

**Effective Date:** 03 October 2026  
**Maintained By:** GDG on Campus Organizing Team  
**Contact:** [gdgoncampustiu@gmail.com](mailto:gdgoncampustiu@gmail.com)

## TL;DR

GDG on Campus is committed to maintaining a respectful, inclusive, safe, and professional environment for everyone participating in the community.

By participating in GDG on Campus community spaces, activities, or events, participants are expected to:

- Treat all community members with respect and dignity.
- Communicate professionally and constructively.
- Respect differences in background, experience, knowledge, and perspectives.
- Refrain from harassment, discrimination, bullying, intimidation, threats, and unwanted conduct.
- Respect personal boundaries, privacy, and consent.
- Give appropriate credit to the work and contributions of others.
- Follow reasonable instructions from organizers, moderators, volunteers, speakers, mentors, venue staff, and other designated representatives.
- Avoid spam, malicious activity, impersonation, disruption, and misuse of community resources.
- Follow applicable event-specific rules, venue policies, campus policies, and applicable laws.
- Report concerns or violations through the designated reporting channels.
- Refrain from retaliation against anyone who makes a good-faith report or participates in the resolution of an incident.

This Code of Conduct applies across **all GDG on Campus community spaces, activities, events, and official representations**, whether online or offline.

If you are unsure whether conduct is appropriate or whether an incident should be reported, you are encouraged to contact a designated community representative for guidance.

---

## 1. Purpose

GDG on Campus is a community built around learning, collaboration, technology, and knowledge sharing.

We want every member to be able to participate, learn, ask questions, share ideas, build projects, attend events, and connect with others in a safe, respectful, inclusive, and professional environment.

This Code of Conduct establishes the standards of behavior expected from everyone participating in the GDG on Campus community and provides a framework for addressing conduct that is inconsistent with those standards.

This Code of Conduct is intended to create a professional community where individuals can disagree, provide feedback, learn from mistakes, and collaborate without fear of harassment, discrimination, intimidation, or abuse.

## 2. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to all GDG on Campus community spaces, activities, and representations, whether online or offline.

This includes, but is not limited to:

- Official community communication channels
- Group chats, discussion channels, forums, and mailing lists
- Social media spaces operated or officially used by the community
- Community websites and online platforms
- Workshops, talks, seminars, meetups, study sessions, and conferences
- Hackathons, coding competitions, and project showcases
- Social gatherings and community-related activities
- Online meetings, webinars, and virtual events
- Communication between community members in connection with GDG on Campus activities
- Venues or locations used for community activities
- Any activity where an individual is officially representing GDG on Campus

The Code of Conduct applies regardless of the platform, location, or format in which the interaction takes place.

It applies to all participants, including but not limited to:

- Community members
- Organizers and volunteers
- Speakers and mentors
- Judges
- Event participants
- Sponsors and partners
- Guests
- Coordinators and representatives

Community leadership is also subject to this Code of Conduct.

## 3. Our Community Standards

### Respectful

Treat others with dignity and respect.

Disagreement is acceptable. Personal attacks, insults, humiliation, hostility, and deliberately disrespectful behavior are not.

### Inclusive

Everyone should be able to participate without being subjected to discrimination or harassment.

We do not tolerate discrimination or harassment based on characteristics including, but not limited to, race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, caste, gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, age, socioeconomic background, appearance, education, experience level, or technical ability.

### Collaborative

Help others learn and grow.

Ask questions in good faith, share knowledge, provide constructive feedback, and recognize the contributions of others.

Differences in experience or technical ability should not be used to belittle or discourage another member.

### Professional

Community spaces should maintain a reasonable standard of professionalism.

Humor, casual conversation, and informal interaction are welcome where appropriate. However, content or behavior that would reasonably make a community member feel unsafe, humiliated, intimidated, or unwelcome is not acceptable.

### Accountable

Members are expected to take responsibility for their actions.

If you make a mistake, acknowledge it, correct it where possible, and respect reasonable requests to stop or modify inappropriate behavior.

## 4. Expected Behavior

Examples of behavior that contribute positively to the community include:

- Being welcoming to new members
- Listening to others and allowing them to express their views
- Respecting different levels of technical knowledge and experience
- Asking questions without fear of being mocked
- Giving constructive and actionable feedback
- Accepting constructive criticism professionally
- Giving appropriate credit to people for their work and contributions
- Collaborating rather than deliberately excluding others
- Respecting personal boundaries
- Obtaining consent before sharing another person's private information, images, recordings, or communications
- Following reasonable instructions from event organizers, moderators, venue staff, or designated community representatives
- Helping maintain a safe and accessible environment
- Reporting concerns when you witness inappropriate behavior

## 5. Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not permitted within the scope of this Code of Conduct.

### 5.1 Harassment and Bullying

Harassment includes unwelcome conduct that targets, intimidates, humiliates, threatens, or creates a hostile environment for another person.

Examples include:

- Repeated unwanted contact
- Personal attacks or targeted insults
- Bullying or intimidation
- Threats of violence or harm
- Deliberately humiliating another person
- Repeatedly targeting an individual after being asked to stop
- Encouraging others to target or harass someone
- Stalking or unwanted following
- Unwelcome sexual attention or advances

### 5.2 Discrimination

Discriminatory behavior, comments, exclusion, or treatment based on protected or personal characteristics is not permitted.

Technical skill, academic background, financial circumstances, previous participation, or familiarity with a particular technology must not be used as a basis for degrading or excluding others.

### 5.3 Sexual Harassment and Sexualized Conduct

The community does not tolerate:

- Unwelcome sexual comments or advances
- Sexualized comments directed at another participant
- Sharing unsolicited sexual content
- Sexual jokes or imagery targeted at individuals
- Unwanted physical contact
- Sexual intimidation or coercion
- Repeated unwanted requests for dates or romantic or sexual interaction

### 5.4 Threats and Violence

Threatening, encouraging, or engaging in physical violence is prohibited.

This includes threats made online or offline.

Any immediate threat to someone's safety may be escalated to appropriate campus authorities, venue security, or emergency services where necessary.

### 5.5 Privacy Violations

Do not share another person's private or sensitive information without their permission.

This includes, but is not limited to:

- Phone numbers
- Personal email addresses
- Home or residential addresses
- Private conversations
- Private photographs or recordings
- Personal documents
- Account credentials
- Authentication tokens or API keys
- Other information that was reasonably understood to be private

Attempting to obtain, expose, distribute, or misuse another person's private information is prohibited.

### 5.6 Unwanted Recording and Photography

Photography, video recording, or audio recording at community activities should respect the reasonable privacy and consent of participants.

Do not intentionally record or publish content involving individuals who have clearly expressed that they do not wish to be recorded, where reasonable accommodation is possible.

Event-specific photography and media guidelines may impose additional requirements.

### 5.7 Disruption

Participants must not deliberately disrupt community activities or prevent others from participating.

Examples include:

- Repeatedly interrupting speakers or presenters
- Deliberately derailing discussions
- Spamming communication channels
- Flooding channels with irrelevant content
- Intentionally interfering with workshops or events
- Disrupting registration, judging, presentations, or other event processes

Constructive disagreement is not considered disruption.

### 5.8 Spam, Abuse, and Malicious Activity

Community spaces must not be used for:

- Spam
- Phishing
- Malware distribution
- Fraud or scams
- Impersonation
- Credential theft
- Malicious links or files
- Unauthorized access attempts
- Deliberate abuse of community infrastructure
- Other activity intended to harm members or the community

### 5.9 Misrepresentation

Members must not falsely represent themselves as organizers, official representatives, sponsors, speakers, partners, or other authorized representatives of GDG on Campus.

Official community communication should only be issued through authorized channels or representatives.

### 5.10 Plagiarism and Misrepresentation of Work

Members should give appropriate credit to the original creators of code, designs, ideas, datasets, research, or other work.

Where an event has specific rules regarding originality, submissions, or reuse of previous work, participants must follow those rules.

Submitting another person's work as your own, deliberately misrepresenting contributions, or concealing required disclosures may result in disciplinary action.

## 6. Communication Standards

Community communication should remain useful, respectful, and reasonably organized.

Members are encouraged to:

- Use the appropriate community space for a discussion
- Keep messages relevant to the purpose of the space
- Avoid unnecessary spam or repeated messages
- Use clear and respectful language
- Ask for clarification before assuming malicious intent
- Resolve disagreements constructively
- Respect requests from moderators regarding community-space organization
- Avoid tagging or contacting individuals excessively
- Avoid unnecessary public disputes

Moderators may remove, restrict, or redirect content when necessary to maintain a functional community space.

The fact that a communication platform is informal does not make the Code of Conduct inapplicable.

## 7. Events and Physical Community Spaces

At GDG on Campus events, participants are expected to follow:

- This Code of Conduct
- Event-specific rules and instructions
- Reasonable instructions from organizers and volunteers
- Venue policies
- Applicable campus policies
- Applicable laws and regulations

Participants should respect:

- Event staff and volunteers
- Speakers and mentors
- Other attendees
- Venue staff
- Equipment and infrastructure
- Accessibility requirements
- Event schedules and procedures

Organizers may take reasonable action to protect the safety and experience of attendees, including asking an individual to stop inappropriate behavior or leave an event.

## 8. Consent and Personal Boundaries

Participation in a community does not imply consent to personal interaction beyond the context of the activity.

Members should respect reasonable boundaries regarding:

- Physical contact
- Personal space
- Photography and recording
- Direct messages and repeated contact
- Romantic or sexual attention
- Sharing personal information
- Participation in activities

If someone asks you to stop a particular behavior, you should respect that request.

A person's continued participation in an event, conversation, or community space should not be interpreted as consent to unwanted interaction.

## 9. Community Leadership and Organizers

Organizers, moderators, volunteers, and other people in positions of responsibility are expected to uphold the same standards as every other participant.

In addition, people representing the community are expected to:

- Act fairly and professionally
- Avoid abusing their position or authority
- Treat reports seriously
- Protect the privacy of people who raise concerns
- Avoid retaliation against individuals who make good-faith reports
- Communicate moderation or disciplinary decisions appropriately
- Apply community rules consistently
- Escalate serious incidents when necessary

Being an organizer, volunteer, speaker, sponsor, or senior member does not provide an exemption from this Code of Conduct.

## 11. Confidentiality and Good-Faith Reports

Reports will be handled with appropriate discretion.

Information from a report will be shared only with people who need it to assess, investigate, respond to, or resolve the matter, subject to applicable requirements and practical limitations.

Good-faith reports will not result in retaliation.

Knowingly submitting false reports, fabricating evidence, or deliberately attempting to misuse the reporting process may itself constitute a violation of this Code of Conduct.

A report does not need to be proven before a concern can be raised.

## 12. How Reports Are Handled

When a report is received, designated community representatives may:

1. Acknowledge and document the report.
2. Assess whether there is an immediate safety concern.
3. Take reasonable steps to protect affected individuals.
4. Gather relevant information from the reporter, the person involved, available witnesses, and available evidence.
5. Determine whether community rules or event policies may have been violated.
6. Take appropriate action based on the circumstances.
7. Communicate the outcome to relevant parties where appropriate and possible.

The community may limit communication about an incident in order to protect privacy.

For serious incidents involving potential criminal conduct, immediate threats, or significant safety concerns, the matter may be escalated to appropriate campus authorities, venue authorities, or law enforcement.

## 13. Enforcement

Violations of this Code of Conduct may result in actions appropriate to the circumstances and severity of the incident.

Possible actions include:

- Informal guidance or clarification
- A request to stop the behavior
- Formal warning
- Removal of content
- Temporary restriction from a community space
- Removal from an event
- Suspension of community participation
- Removal from an organizing or volunteer role
- Restriction from future events
- Removal from the community
- Escalation to relevant institutional or legal authorities where appropriate

Actions do not necessarily need to follow this order.

The community may consider factors including:

- Severity of the conduct
- Impact on others
- Whether the behavior was repeated
- Whether the person complied after being asked to stop
- Whether there was an immediate safety concern
- Relevant context and available evidence
- Previous violations, where applicable

Serious violations may result in immediate removal from an event or community space.

## 14. No Retaliation

Retaliation against a person for:

- Making a good-faith report
- Participating in an investigation
- Supporting another person's report
- Providing relevant information
- Raising a legitimate concern

is prohibited.

Retaliation may result in separate disciplinary action.

## 15. Disagreements and Constructive Debate

GDG on Campus is a technical and learning community. Differences of opinion are expected and healthy.

Members are free to:

- Disagree with technical approaches
- Challenge ideas
- Critique projects
- Debate architectural decisions
- Question community decisions
- Provide constructive feedback

However, disagreement must remain focused on ideas, decisions, or work rather than becoming a personal attack.

Criticize the idea, not the person.

A disagreement is not, by itself, a Code of Conduct violation. The manner in which the disagreement is conducted may be.

## 16. Event-Specific Rules

Individual events may have additional rules covering matters such as:

- Registration and eligibility
- Attendance
- Project submissions
- Intellectual property
- Team formation
- Judging
- Sponsorship requirements
- Photography and media
- Venue access
- Safety
- Equipment usage
- Competition integrity
- Deadlines

Event-specific rules supplement this Code of Conduct and do not replace it.

Where an event-specific rule imposes a higher standard for a particular activity, participants are expected to follow that rule.

## 17. Community Responsibility

A healthy community is a shared responsibility.

Members are encouraged to:

- Look out for one another
- Intervene safely when appropriate
- Report serious or concerning behavior
- Support people who may be experiencing harassment or discrimination
- Avoid amplifying harmful behavior
- Give others an opportunity to learn from reasonable mistakes

Members should not put themselves at risk when responding to an incident.

If a situation involves an immediate threat to someone's safety, seek appropriate assistance rather than attempting to handle the situation alone.

## 18. Applicability to Representatives

This Code of Conduct also applies when an individual is representing GDG on Campus outside a formal community event.

Examples include:

- Speaking on behalf of the community
- Representing the community at another event
- Using an official community account
- Communicating with sponsors or partners as a community representative
- Representing GDG on Campus in an official capacity

The community's name or affiliation must not be used to justify conduct that violates this Code of Conduct.

## 19. Updates to This Code

This Code of Conduct may be reviewed and updated by the GDG on Campus organizing team when necessary.

Significant changes should be communicated to the community through appropriate official channels.

The latest published version is the version applicable to community activities, subject to any event-specific rules communicated to participants.

## 20. Acknowledgement

By participating in GDG on Campus activities or community spaces, you are expected to read, understand, and follow this Code of Conduct.

Participation in the community is a privilege shared with the responsibility to help maintain a respectful, safe, inclusive, and constructive environment for everyone.
